# Staging and tension for rules-story

Use for horror picture planning inside the saved identity's frame. Start from approved narration and
measured alignment windows. Keep sentence-boundary hard cuts, phone readability and the series kit;
the techniques here do not prescribe shot counts, effects, sound quotas or another storyboard artifact.

## Make the danger readable

Establish the viewpoint, threat and possible exit where those matter. Keep doorways, props, figures
and screen directions consistent across cuts. Let the audience understand enough geography to
anticipate an encounter; conceal the threat deliberately rather than through an unreadable picture.

For each meaningful beat, decide what the viewer knows, what remains hidden and which visible
change directs attention. A hand stopping short of a handle, a chair turning without an occupant or
a shadow crossing the exit conveys more than a label saying FEAR. Choose physical details that
carry this story; not every shot needs a character, bodily reaction or environmental effect.

Use one clear focal subject at a time. Position, scale, contrast, an obstruction or a moving prop can
direct the gaze. Retain enough context that a close-up does not erase the danger's location.
Darkness must leave the important action legible within the existing black-ground series palette.

## Move and cut for a reason

A push may reveal a newly moving handle; reframing may uncover the blocked exit. If no information
or emotional relationship changes, a deliberate hold may serve the beat better. Name what ends
the hold: the latch clicks, the figure moves or the narration reveals a consequence.

Receive the viewer's attention across cuts: a handle on the right should lead to a recognizable door
or clearly re-established viewpoint. A changed viewpoint is allowed; accidental geography is not.
Fit actions and pauses into actual narration windows. Do not shorten the approved recording to fit a
stock rhythm ladder or keep an idle picture behind several unrelated sentences. Optional sound cues
belong in the existing pipeline mix and must respect the identity's sound signature.

## Worked sequence: the corridor door

This is an illustrative plan for fictional narration, not approved copy or measured timing.
Replace its anchors with the actual script and alignment times when used.

- Setup, on the sentence identifying the door: show the figure in the corridor, the door to their right
  and the exit behind them. Establish the handle and the figure's reaching hand.
- Anomaly, on “the handle turns”: cut at the sentence boundary to the same handle, retaining its
  orientation. It rotates before the hand touches it. A small push makes the separation unmistakable.
- Reaction, on the instruction to step back: show the hand withdraw and the figure retreat toward
  the established exit. Hold only while the expected next movement remains the focus.
- Consequence, on the next reveal: cut wide enough to show that the exit is now blocked. The new
  obstruction changes the geography visibly; the viewer should not mistake it for a continuity error.

The sequence earns tension through anticipation and consequence. Build and inspect its actual
intermediate frames and transitions; a written plan is not evidence that the motion works.
