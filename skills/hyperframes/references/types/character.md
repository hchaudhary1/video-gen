# Characters and continuity

Use acting to make the event readable: attention, anticipation, action, contact, reaction and settle.
For horror, restraint, a delayed reaction or an intentionally wrong movement may carry more tension
than an exaggerated take. Match the saved profile and scene's visual language.

- Keep a recurring figure's proportions, silhouette, clothes and detail consistent across shots.
  Normal human figures have two arms and two legs; re-pose existing limbs rather than adding copies.
- Offset connected parts naturally: head notices, hand reaches, body follows. Avoid identical curves
  on every limb or crowd member unless synchronized movement is the intended anomaly.
- Feet meet the floor, hands meet the prop, eyes look at the threat. Preserve these relationships
  through motion and at the seam, not only in a posed still.
- Squash, overshoot, a blink or an idle breath are choices. A face may snap unnaturally when that is
  the authored scare; accidental discontinuities need repair.
- Hold a reaction long enough to understand it. Let the picture add the event while the required
  series captions remain readable.

Build rigs from keyed parameters: position, arm angles, gaze, mouth and pose states over scene time.
Share rigs/helpers across scenes when useful; scope each instance and register its scene timeline
last. Seed line boil or jitter from time/frame index, so a held pose redraws identically at the same
timestamp. See [runtimes](../runtimes.md) for seekable drawing.

Inspect anticipation, contact, extremes and settle, then play the action. Correct the rig or source
drawing; an overlay that leaves the old hand or arm underneath creates a second defect.
