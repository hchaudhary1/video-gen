# Visual planning against the recording

Optional working guidance for the director's beat plan. Keep the plan with existing project/job
artifacts; this does not add mandatory `STORYLINES.md`, `SPEC.md` or `STORYBOARD.md` documents,
another approval gate, or a replacement for `film.json`.

Start from the approved script, saved profile, identity and measured narration. For an existing film,
read prior decisions and preserve successful details. Original fiction can invent events and lore;
reused archival material and factual claims still need provenance. Do not imply that a real photograph
documents a fictional event.

## Make each beat concrete

For each meaningful sentence or action, identify:

- The exact spoken anchor and its measured time window.
- What appears on screen: recognizable subject, props, geography and scale.
- What changes: setup, action, contact, reaction or consequence.
- What the viewer should notice first, and how staging or motion directs attention.
- The ending state and how it leads to the next picture.
- Any sound cue, routed through the pipeline mix.

A useful line might be: “On ‘the handle turns’, close on the same corridor door; its handle rotates,
the figure's hand stops short, and the next cut reveals the empty other side.” Use actual alignment
times in the working plan, rather than estimating from words per second.

Choose scene boundaries for the material. A scene file may contain several beats or cover the whole
recording; a new sentence does not require another file. A Short recomposes one rule as its own trigger,
escalation and loop while preserving its parent's identity and number.

## Establish a coherent picture

Use the identity and kit for the frame. Specify choices concretely where they matter: palette values,
local font and weight, subject scale, light direction, motion timing and focal point. Darkness should
leave meaningful subjects legible at phone size. Glow and grain support the series, but must not erase
a hand, facial reaction or exhibit detail.

Maintain recurring subjects, proportions, prop placement and screen geography. An archival exhibit,
diagram, silhouette, room, typography or pictogram can serve the same film when the transition is clear.
Choose representations for what the viewer needs to understand, rather than substituting floating cards
for every concrete event.

Use the Motion Explainer catalogs to consider camera, editing and graphic options across the film.
Let the scene motivate the choice. Holds are useful when an impending change is readable; idle drift
behind several unrelated sentences is not a substitute for new pictures.

## When stills help

If requested, create a board from real built scenes using pipeline snapshots. Keep it separate from
generated root files. Label planned frames as plans; inspect actual snapshot files before claiming they
show the intended shot. Follow the project's existing human checkpoints for decisions.
